One connected system

Google, Maps, Meta, website, CRM: linked by the pixel

Today Premier's ads, profile, website and customer list don't talk to each other, so nobody can say which dollar booked which job. After the launch they all report into one place, and every booked job teaches the ads who to find next.

The dashed lines are the growth loop. Each booked job makes the next ad dollar smarter.

The pixel, in plain words

A pixel is a few lines of code from Meta (and a matching tag from Google) placed on Premier's website. When someone visits, it notes which ad or post they came from and what they did: watched the video, opened the services page, tapped call, sent the estimate form.

The CRM adds the last step the website can't see: whether that lead became a paid job and for how much. That result is sent back to Google and Meta through a secure server connection (Meta's Conversions API, Google's offline conversions). The ads then stop optimizing for clicks and start optimizing for booked jobs.

The data stays in Premier's own accounts, and the site shows a short privacy notice.

What the link makes possible

  • Cost per booked job for every channel, so spend moves to what works
  • Retargeting: someone who looked at roof cleaning but didn't call sees Premier's video for the next 30 days
  • Look-alikes: Meta finds new homeowners who resemble Premier's best past customers
  • Re-booking: last year's customers get the annual-rinse offer at the right time
  • Proof for Brandon: one report from lead source to dollars

Where the money is

A page for every neighborhood that pays best

The CRM ranks neighborhoods by revenue and profit per job. The top ones each get their own page, their own ad audience and their own door hangers, so Premier shows up first exactly where jobs are worth the most.

How a neighborhood page works

  • A page such as "House washing in Sun City Hilton Head" with real jobs from that community
  • Reviews from neighbors, the community's HOA rules on exterior cleaning, and typical home types
  • Google and Meta ads aimed at that neighborhood land on its own page, so the message matches
  • "We're on your street this week" door hangers with a QR code to the same page
  • Every lead is tagged with the neighborhood in the CRM

Picking the neighborhoods

  • Week 2: past jobs imported and grouped by neighborhood
  • Rank by total revenue, average job value and repeat rate
  • Build the top 3 by day 90, then 2 a month in the Growth plan
  • Likely first candidates to check against the data: Sun City Hilton Head, Hampton Lake, Palmetto Dunes, Sea Pines, Palmetto Bluff
  • Pages that bring the most booked jobs get more ad budget

Term

An initial 90-day launch term from the start date. After that, the monthly plan continues month to month until either party gives 30 days' written notice.

Payment

The $1,400 setup is due at signing. Monthly plan invoices are due within 7 days. Work pauses if an invoice is more than 15 days late. Pass-through costs are paid by Premier directly.

Ownership of work

When each invoice is paid in full, Premier owns the final films, photos, logos, website files and written content delivered under it, and receives clean, unwatermarked masters. Until then, previews carry the Above All Media watermark. Above All Media may show the work in its portfolio unless Premier asks in writing that it not.

Accounts stay in Premier's name

The domain, Google Business Profile, Google Ads, Google Analytics, Meta business portfolio, ad accounts, Pixel and CRM are owned by Premier. Above All Media is added as a manager or partner and removes its own access when the agreement ends.

Premier's part

Give account access in the first week, approve content within 3 business days (silence counts as approval for routine posts), provide job-site access for film days, and send real job photos when possible.

Likeness and release

Brandon Williams, Jerahmy Sorensen and any crew who appear on camera agree that their name, image and voice may be used in Premier's marketing. Customers and their homes appear only with permission. Footage of partner businesses (for example Local Legend and SERG Group) is used only with their written approval.

Music and media

Music, footage and fonts are original or properly licensed. Spots shown on restaurant screens or broadcast use music licensed for public playback.

No ranking guarantee

Google and Meta decide rankings and reach. Above All Media commits to doing the work in this agreement on schedule and reporting real results every month; it does not guarantee a specific rank, number of leads or revenue. The projections shared with Premier are estimates, not promises.

Customer data and messages

Premier owns its customer list. Above All Media uses it only to run Premier's marketing, keeps it private, and returns or deletes its copies when the agreement ends. Marketing emails include an unsubscribe link and Premier's address; texts go only to customers who agreed to receive them.

Confidentiality

Each party keeps the other's customer lists, pricing and business information private.

Ending the agreement

On notice, Above All Media finishes or hands over work in progress, delivers all paid-for files, and confirms Premier holds every account. Fees paid for completed months are not refunded.

Liability

Each party's total liability under this agreement is limited to the fees paid in the 3 months before a claim, except for unpaid fees or misuse of the other's confidential information.

Governing law

South Carolina law applies. The parties will try to settle any disagreement in good faith before taking other action.
