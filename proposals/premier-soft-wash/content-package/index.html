2 · Return

The return, month by month

The new plan costs $752 a month more than today. Each extra booked job adds about $225 of profit ($450 average job at a 50% margin), so the system covers its extra cost at about 3.3 extra jobs a month. After that, every job is profit.

3 · Lead quality

More profitable leads, and more commercial work

Not every lead is worth the same. A one-time driveway wash is a $450 job. A homeowner on a yearly rebook is worth three times that over three years, and a restaurant or storefront on a monthly plan is worth many times more, with no ad cost to win the second visit. The Local Legend film is the proof piece for commercial buyers: SERG runs 18 restaurants across Hilton Head and Bluffton, and every property manager and owner who sees it sees Premier on a real commercial job.

Assumes a $300 monthly exterior plan per location (storefront, patio, windows, dumpster pad). Premier sets the real price. A yearly rebook assumes one $450 job a year; a homeowner who also books windows and gutters is worth more. These are planning figures, not guarantees.

5 · Enterprise value

Building a business worth more when it's time to sell

Small service businesses are usually valued at a multiple of the owner's yearly profit, often around 2 to 3 times. Buyers pay toward the top of that range, or above it, when revenue is recurring, the customer list and lead sources are documented, and the business runs without the owners on every job. Every dollar of yearly profit the system adds is worth roughly two and a half to three and a half dollars on the day Premier is sold.

What buyers pay more for, and what builds it
  • Recurring revenueCommercial monthly plans and yearly rebooks in the CRM
